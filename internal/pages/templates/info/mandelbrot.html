The Mandelbrot set is a shape in the complex plane drawn by a rule that fits on one line. Pick a complex number c, start from zero and repeat the same step again and again:

zn+1 = zn2 +c, z0=0

If the numbers stay near the origin forever, c belongs to the set; if they run away to infinity, it does not. Once a number is farther than 2 from the origin, it is sure to escape, so a computer can stop early:

|zn| >2 ⇒ zn→∞

History

Around 1918 the French mathematicians Pierre Fatou and Gaston Julia studied what happens when a function of a complex number is applied again and again. They had no computers and could only imagine the pictures. In 1978 Robert Brooks and Peter Matelski printed the first rough image of the set, made of text characters. Benoit Mandelbrot, working at IBM, made far better pictures in 1980 and showed how intricate the boundary is. Adrien Douady and John Hubbard proved the first deep facts about the set and named it after him, and an article by A. K. Dewdney in Scientific American in August 1985 made it famous far beyond mathematics.

Interesting facts

  • The set is connected: it is a single piece, even though thin filaments are all that join some of its parts.
  • Its largest part is a heart-shaped cardioid. The round disk to its left has its center at −1 and a radius of 1/4; there the numbers settle into a cycle of two values.
  • On the real line the set covers exactly the interval from −2 to 1/4.
  • The boundary is so wrinkled that its fractal (Hausdorff) dimension is 2, as for a filled area (Mitsuhiro Shishikura, 1998). The area itself is about 1.5066, and no exact formula is known.
  • Zooming into the boundary reveals endless small copies of the whole set.
  • Every point of the set gives a connected Julia set; outside the set the Julia set breaks into dust.
  • Whether the set is locally connected is a famous open question.

How Image89 draws it

Every pixel center becomes c, and the step repeats until the number escapes or the iteration limit of the preset is reached, 256 by default. Points that never escape take the first color of the palette. Escaped points are colored by a smooth count, so colors change gradually instead of in bands:

μ=n+1− log2⁡ log⁡|zn|

The default view is centered at −0.75 and is 3.5 wide and 2.5 high. For other proportions the visible area grows, so the figure is never stretched. The preset lives in the [types.mandelbrot] section of the config.
